Profession Basics

What is a profession?

  • A profession is a job for your character, like tailor or skinner. It lets you collect materials or make items.
  • You can have 2 main jobs. Cooking, First Aid and Fishing are extras anyone can learn. They don't count toward your 2.
  • Each job has a skill number that starts at 1 and goes up to 300. It goes up by 1 when you make or collect something.
  • If you drop a job, its skill goes back to 0. Choose carefully.
  • Rogues: learn First Aid. You can't heal yourself, so bandages are your best friend. Learn it in Darnassus at level 10 and make bandages from the cloth humanoids drop.

How to start one

  1. Go to a big city (Darnassus for Night Elves). Ask any guard about professions and pick the one you want. The guard marks the trainer on your map.
  2. Talk to the trainer and learn the profession. It costs almost nothing.
  3. Open your spellbook (P) and click the profession's icon. Drag the icon to your action bar so it's easy to open next time.
  4. Pick a recipe. If you have the materials in your bags, click Create.
  5. Your skill goes up. Keep going, and visit the trainer now and then for new recipes.

Recipe colors

Each recipe in your profession window has a color. It tells you if making it will raise your skill.

  • Orange always gives a point.
  • Yellow usually gives a point.
  • Green only sometimes gives a point.
  • Grey never does. Move on to a new recipe.

The routes on this page pick recipes that stay orange or yellow, so you don't waste materials.

Why did my skill stop going up?

Your skill has a ceiling. When you hit it, the number stops, even on orange recipes. To raise the ceiling, go back to the trainer and learn the next rank.

Apprentice1 → 75Any level
Journeyman75 → 150Level 10
Expert150 → 225Level 20
Artisan225 → 300Level 35

Example: Your Tailoring shows 75 / 75, and you're level 12. You go to the trainer and learn Journeyman. Now it shows 75 / 150, and you can keep going.

The level under each rank is your character level, not your skill. If you're too low a level, keep questing and come back. For gathering jobs (Skinning, Herbalism, Mining), you can learn the next rank a bit early, at skill 50, 125 and 200.

Gathering and crafting

Gathering means collecting raw materials out in the world: skinning dead animals (Skinning), picking flowers (Herbalism) or mining rocks (Mining). It's easy, you do it while questing, and you can sell what you collect.

Crafting means turning materials into useful items: clothes (Tailoring), leather armor (Leatherworking), enchants (Enchanting), potions (Alchemy), metal armor and weapons (Blacksmithing), or gadgets (Engineering). Crafting uses a lot of materials.

That's why people often pick one of each: the gathering job collects what the crafting job needs, so you don't have to buy it.

Pairs that work well

Skinning + Leatherworking
Best for a rogue · gathering feeds crafting Rogues wear leather, so Leatherworking makes your own armor and armor kits. It needs well over 1,000 pieces of leather to reach 300. Skinning gets it for free while you quest.
Tailoring + Enchanting
No gathering needed Cloth drops from humanoid enemies. Enchanting breaks your extra Tailoring items into enchanting materials, so the two feed each other. Tailoring makes cloth gear, so it's less useful for a rogue, but Enchanting helps every class.
Herbalism + Alchemy
Gathering feeds craftingHerbs become potions and elixirs. Useful for every class and always in demand.
Mining + Blacksmithing or Engineering
Gathering feeds craftingOre becomes plate and mail armor, weapons, or Engineering gadgets and bombs. Blacksmiths also make the sharpening stones rogues use.
Two gathering professions
Best for goldSkinning with Herbalism or Mining. Sell everything you collect. You can swap one for a crafting job later.
